## 5 Proof of Gauss's Theorem and Its Applications

### 5.1 Rigorous Proof of Gauss's Theorem

Gauss's theorem, recognized as the divergence theorem, represents pivotal mathematical instrument in advanced vector analysis and theoretical physics:

$$\oint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \int_V \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dV \tag{5-1}$$

Where $S$ represents closed surface enclosing volume $V$, and $\mathbf{F}$ denotes arbitrary continuously differentiable vector field.

### 5.2 Rigorous Mathematical Proof

To establish mathematical rigor, we commence from precise divergence definition:

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \lim_{\Delta V \to 0} \frac{\oint_{\Delta S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}}{\Delta V} \tag{5-2}$$

Dividing volume $V$ into infinite collection of infinitesimal volume elements $\Delta V_i$:

$$\int_V \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dV = \sum_i \int_{\Delta V_i} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dV \approx \sum_i \oint_{\Delta S_i} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} \tag{5-3}$$

On interfaces between volume elements, surface integrals cancel, leaving only surface integral of entire closed surface $S$:

$$\sum_i \oint_{\Delta S_i} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \oint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} \tag{5-4}$$

As volume elements approach zero, approximate equality becomes exact equality, completing proof.

### 5.3 Rigorous Application in Gravitational Field Theory

Within spatial dynamics framework, gravitational field vector field $\mathbf{g}$ exhibits fundamental geometric properties satisfying Gauss's theorem conditions:

$$\oint_S \mathbf{g} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \int_V \nabla \cdot \mathbf{g} \, dV \tag{5-5}$$

Combining with gravitational field divergence formula (4-5):

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{g} = -4\pi G \rho \tag{5-6}$$

Substituting into Gauss's theorem:

$$\oint_S \mathbf{g} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = -4\pi G \int_V \rho \, dV = -4\pi G M \tag{5-7}$$

Where $M$ is total mass enclosed by closed surface $S$, consistent with Newton's universal gravitation law conclusion.
