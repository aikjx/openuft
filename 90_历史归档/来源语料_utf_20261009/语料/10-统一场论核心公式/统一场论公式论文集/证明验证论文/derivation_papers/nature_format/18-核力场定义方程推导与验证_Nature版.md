## Derivation of the Nuclear Force Field Equation

### Step 1: Fundamental Definition

In UFT, the nuclear force field \( \mathbf{D} \) is defined as the time derivative of the mass field. For a mass \( m \), we define:

$$\mathbf{D} = -g m \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\mathbf{R}}{r^3}\right)$$ 

The negative sign indicates the direction of the force, while \( g \) is the nuclear coupling constant.

### Step 2: Total Differential Expansion

Expanding the time derivative using the product rule:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\mathbf{R}}{r^3}\right) = \frac{1}{r^3}\frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt} + \mathbf{R}\frac{d}{dt}\left(r^{-3}\right)$$

### Step 3: Component Calculations

According to the spacetime unification equation, the time derivative of the position vector equals the speed of light vector:

$$\frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt} = \mathbf{C}$$

Thus, the first term becomes:

$$\frac{1}{r^3}\frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt} = \frac{\mathbf{C}}{r^3}$$

For the second term, we calculate the time derivative of \( r^{-3} \):

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(r^{-3}\right) = -3r^{-4}\frac{dr}{dt} = -\frac{3\dot{r}}{r^4}$$

where \( \dot{r} = \frac{dr}{dt} \) is the radial velocity. The second term becomes:

$$\mathbf{R}\frac{d}{dt}\left(r^{-3}\right) = -\frac{3\mathbf{R}\dot{r}}{r^4}$$

### Step 4: Final Simplification

Combining and simplifying the terms:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\mathbf{R}}{r^3}\right) = \frac{\mathbf{C}}{r^3} - \frac{3\mathbf{R}\dot{r}}{r^4} = \frac{1}{r^3}\left(\mathbf{C} - 3\frac{\mathbf{R}}{r}\dot{r}\right)$$

Substituting back into the definition of \( \mathbf{D} \):

$$\mathbf{D} = -g m \frac{1}{r^3}\left(\mathbf{C} - 3\frac{\mathbf{R}}{r}\dot{r}\right) = -\frac{g m}{r^3}\left(\mathbf{C} - 3\frac{\mathbf{R}}{r}\dot{r}\right)$$
