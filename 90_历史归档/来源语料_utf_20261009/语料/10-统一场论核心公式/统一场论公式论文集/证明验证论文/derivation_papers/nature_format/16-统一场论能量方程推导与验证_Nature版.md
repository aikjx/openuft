## Abstract

The Unified Field Theory (UFT) energy equation reinterprets energy essence from spatial wave dynamics and geometric properties, extending Einstein's mass-energy equivalence with deeper physical insights. The equation $$e = m_0 c^2 = mc^2\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$ reveals intrinsic connections between energy, mass, spatial motion, and relativistic effects. Through symbolic computation with SymPy, numerical validation, and comparison with relativistic formulas, we verify its mathematical consistency and explore its significance in revealing energy essence, understanding field unity, and advancing physics grand unification.

### 3.1 Fundamental Principles

The derivation relies on core principles of Unified Field Theory:

### 3.2 Derivation Steps

**Step 1: Spatial Wave Equation and Energy Essence**

According to UFT, space around objects diffuses outward in spiral motion, described by the wave equation:

$$\nabla^2 \phi - \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial t^2} = 0$$ 

where \(\phi\) is the spatial wave potential and \(c\) is the speed of light. Energy is fundamentally a manifestation of spatial waves, establishing a direct connection between energy and spatial dynamics.

**Step 2: Mass-Energy Relationship from Spatial Perspective**

From the UFT mass definition, mass \(m\) is a measure of spatial motion quantity:

$$m = \oint \rho_v \cdot dV$$ 

where \(\rho_v\) is the spatial motion density. Combining with Einstein's mass-energy equivalence, we establish the energy-spatial motion relationship:

$$e = mc^2$$ 

**Step 3: Relativistic Effects and Spatial Geometry**

In relativity, mass increases with velocity:

$$m = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$$ 

From UFT perspective, this arises from changes in spatial wave patterns around moving objects:
- Spherical spatial waves for stationary objects
- Compressed waves in the direction of motion
- Spatial geometric changes causing relativistic mass effects

**Step 4: UFT Energy Equation Formulation**

Substituting relativistic mass into the mass-energy relationship yields relativistic energy:

$$e_{rel} = mc^2 = \frac{m_0 c^2}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$$ 

However, from UFT's spatial essence perspective, we derive the energy equation:

$$e = m_0 c^2 = mc^2\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$ 

This equation reveals that intrinsic energy remains constant regardless of reference frame, with relativistic effects manifesting as apparent mass changes.
