Our approach is founded on two key postulates:

1. **Mass as Quantum Geometric Flux**: We propose that mass corresponds to the quantum flux of spacetime geometry through a solid angle:

2. **Planck Mass as Complete Solid Angle Flux**: The Planck mass represents the quantum geometric flux through a complete solid angle (4π steradians):
   \begin{equation}
   m_p = k \cdot 4\pi
   \end{equation}
   This interpretation provides a physical meaning to the Planck mass, connecting it to the fundamental quantum structure of spacetime.

### Quantum Geometric Expression for G

From our postulates, we derive a novel expression for the gravitational constant. Expressing \( k \) in terms of the Planck mass from Equation 2:

\begin{equation}
k = \frac{m_p}{4\pi}
\end{equation}

Substituting this into the standard quantum gravity expression for \( G \) in terms of the Planck mass:

\begin{equation}
G = \frac{\hbar c}{m_p^2}
\end{equation}

This yields our central result—the quantum geometric expression for the gravitational constant:

\begin{equation}
G = \frac{16\pi^2 \hbar c}{k^2}
\end{equation}

Substituting into Equation 5 gives \( G_{\text{calc}} \approx 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{m}^3·\text{kg}^{-1}·\text{s}^{-2} \), which agrees with the experimental value with a relative error of approximately \( 6 \times 10^{-5} \).

### Fundamental G-c Relationship

Equation 5 reveals a critical proportional relationship:
