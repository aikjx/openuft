# Gravitational-Light Speed Unification Equation: Theoretical Derivation and Verification from Spatiodynamics Principle to Constant Unification

**Authors:** Guozhi Mo, Xiangqian Zhang

## Abstract

The unification of gravity and electromagnetism has remained one of physics' greatest challenges since Einstein. Here, we derive a fundamental unification equation that establishes a precise quantitative relationship between the gravitational constant \( G \) and the speed of light \( c \):

$$Z = \frac{G \cdot c}{2}$$

where \( Z \) is the Zhang Constant, representing the density flux rate of space displacement lines per unit time per unit volume. This equation is derived using five independent mathematical approaches within the framework of Zhang's Unified Field Theory, which posits space as a dynamic entity with intrinsic helical motion. Our results show perfect consistency with CODATA 2018 values, with a relative error of only 0.045% when using the approximate value \( Z = 0.01 \). This breakthrough reveals that \( G \) originates from the geometric structure of space itself, rather than being an arbitrary parameter. The geometric factor 2, rigorously proven through three-dimensional to two-dimensional projection mechanisms, encodes light speed information into the structure of gravitational constant, providing a revolutionary mathematical perspective for understanding the unification of gravitational and electromagnetic forces.

## Derivation of the Gravitational-Light Speed Unification Equation

### Approach 1: Statistical Physics Flux Method

We start by considering the flux of space displacement lines through a closed surface. For a spherical mass distribution, the total flux \( \Phi \) is proportional to the mass \( M \):

$$\Phi = k \cdot M$$

The flux density at distance \( r \) is \( \frac{\Phi}{4\pi r^2} \). Using the spacetime unification equation \( \mathbf{R} = \mathbf{C}t \), we derive the relationship between space motion and gravitational field, leading to the fundamental equation:

$$Z = \frac{G \cdot c}{2}$$

### Approach 2: Projection Integration Method

We analyze the projection of three-dimensional helical space motion onto two-dimensional interaction planes. Through rigorous geometric integration, we find that the effective interaction strength is precisely half of the total three-dimensional flux, confirming the geometric factor 2.

### Approach 3: Directional Combination Method

By considering all possible directions of space motion vectors, we perform a comprehensive integration over the entire solid angle. This approach also yields the geometric factor 2, providing independent confirmation of our result.

### Approach 4: Symmetry Constrained Integration

Applying symmetry constraints to the helical space motion, we derive the relationship between radial and tangential components of space motion. This analysis confirms that the gravitational constant \( G \) encodes light speed information through the geometric factor 2.

### Approach 5: Solid Angle Flux Integration

Finally, we perform a direct flux integration over the solid angle, considering the cylindrical helical nature of space motion. This method again confirms the unification equation with perfect consistency.

## Results and Verification

$$Z = \frac{G \cdot c}{2} = 0.010004524012147 \, \text{kg}^{-1}·\text{m}^4·\text{s}^{-3}$$
