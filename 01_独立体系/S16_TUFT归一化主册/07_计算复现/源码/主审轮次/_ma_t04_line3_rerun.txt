[4] CLASSICAL NONLINEARITY = DETERMINISTIC EVOLUTION
================================================================================

  The Skyrme EOM (variation of L = L2 + L4) is a nonlinear hyperbolic
  PDE for U(x,t).  Given smooth initial data U(x,0) and dU/dt(x,0) on a
  Cauchy surface, the classical Cauchy problem has a UNIQUE solution
  forward in time (well-posedness for semilinear wave equations, modulo
  known blow-up caveats which are themselves deterministic).

  Contrast table:

                      property  classical nonlinear PDE  quantum measurement (collapse)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
                 evolution law       deterministic PDE  non-deterministic (Born)
                   state space  field U(x) in C^inf(S^3)     Hilbert space |psi>
                 superposition  NOT preserved (nonlinear)  preserved (unitary part)
                   probability                    NONE  |amp|^2 -> probability
                     unitarity       energy conserving  BROKEN during collapse
            time-reversibility         yes (classical)      no (arrow of time)
              outcome given IC                  unique     stochastic ensemble

  The nonlinearity in L4 makes the EOM nonlinear.  Nonlinearity in a
  classical PDE means: superposition of two solutions is NOT a solution.
  It does NOT mean: the solution randomly jumps between alternatives.
  It does NOT mean: probabilities emerge.

[5] LINEAR vs NONLINEAR EOM: numerical contrast (toy 1D)
================================================================================

  Toy model: 1D field phi(x,t) on a ring.
    Linear part (L2 analog):  phi_tt - phi_xx = 0   (wave eqn)
    Nonlinear part (L4 analog): phi_tt - phi_xx - lambda phi^3 = 0
  We integrate both from the SAME smooth initial data and show:
    (a) both are deterministic (no randomness)
    (b) nonlinear solution does NOT 'collapse' to one branch
    (c) energy is conserved in both

  Initial data: two smooth Gaussian bumps at x=1.0 and x=5.0.
  Integrated to t = 2.0 ring units (CFL dt = 0.1 dx).

                quantity    linear (lam=0)  nonlinear (lam=0.3)
  ----------------------------------------------------------
               E_initial      3.223079e+00      3.274564e+00
                 E_final      3.226517e+00      3.242613e+00
            |dE/E| drift      1.066644e-03      9.757419e-03
         max|phi| at t=2      9.997222e-01      1.024030e+00

  OBSERVATION:
  - Both runs evolve DETERMINISTICALLY from the same initial data.
  - Energy is conserved in both (drift ~ leapfrog discretization).
  - The nonlinear solution DIFFERS from the linear one (nonlinear
    interaction phase shifts, waveform distortion).
  - BUT: nowhere does the nonlinear solution RANDOMLY pick one bump
    and erase the other.  There is NO projection, NO Born rule, NO
    measurement outcome.  Both bumps keep evolving together.

  This is the essential point: L4 nonlinearity bends the wave, it does
  NOT collapse it.  The classical field evolves as a single deterministic
  configuration; there is no ensemble of alternatives to choose from.

[6] BOUNDARY ANALYSIS: how far does M3 hold in TUFT?
================================================================================

  M3 (linear -> nonlinear) as a measurement-problem requirement:
    To explain wavefunction collapse, one needs a dynamics that
    interpolates between unitary linear evolution (Schrodinger) and
    non-deterministic probabilistic reduction (Born rule).

  TUFT classical field theory provides:
    (a) a NONLINEAR classical PDE for U(x,t)  -- YES, L4 does this
    (b) a Hilbert space |psi>                  -- NO, absent
    (c) a probability amplitude                -- NO, absent
    (d) a Born rule |amp|^2 = probability       -- NO, absent
    (e) a non-deterministic reduction event    -- NO, EOM is deterministic

  Therefore M3 is PARTIALLY satisfied:
    - The letter 'nonlinear' is present (L4 makes EOM nonlinear).
    - The spirit 'nonlinear dynamics that produces probabilistic
      outcomes from a superposition' is ABSENT.

  TUFT's nonlinearity is the WRONG TYPE of nonlinearity for M3.
  Skyrme L4 nonlinearity is:
    - gauge-invariant, Lorentz-invariant, energy-conserving
    - classical-field nonlinearity (analogous to phi^4 self-interaction)
    - responsible for Derrick stability (finite R*)

  What M3 would actually require (e.g. Weinberg nonlinear quantum
  mechanics, GRW spontaneous collapse, Penrose-Diosi):
    - nonlinearity in the SCHRODINGER equation for |psi>
    - stochastic or non-unitary term
    - explicit coupling to a 'measurement' pointer basis
    - Born rule as an output (or postulate)
  NONE of these exist in TUFT's classical field Lagrangian.
