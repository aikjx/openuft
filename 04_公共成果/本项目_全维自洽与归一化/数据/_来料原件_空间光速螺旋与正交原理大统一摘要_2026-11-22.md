# Breaking the Grand Unification of Forces via State-of-the-Art Methods: The Space Light-Speed Spiral and Orthogonality Principle

The grand unification of fundamental forces remains one of the most long-standing core objectives in theoretical physics. Conventional unification frameworks mostly rely on gauge symmetry extension, extra-dimensional compactification, or supersymmetry, yet they still face persistent obstacles including the hierarchy problem, the failure to naturally incorporate gravity, and unresolved experimental inconsistencies.

This work proposes an alternative constructive approach built on **the space light-speed spiral** and **the orthogonality (perpendicularity) principle**, aiming to unify the four fundamental interactions from a geometric-dynamical first-principle perspective.

The core postulate states that the intrinsic propagation of spacetime itself follows a light-speed spiral motion. Rather than treating light as a passive wave traveling inside a fixed background spacetime, the spiral dynamics of spacetime at speed c becomes the primordial geometric origin. Under this configuration, the **orthogonality principle** defines the mutual perpendicular relation between the spiral tangent direction, normal curvature direction, and torsion axis. Different fundamental forces emerge as distinct projection components of this unified spiral geometric field, corresponding to curvature, torsion, and angular frequency degrees of freedom respectively.

Within this formalism, gravitational interaction is associated with the curvature component of the light-speed spiral, while the strong, weak and electromagnetic gauge interactions originate from the orthogonal torsion and rotational degrees of the same spiral manifold. The gauge group structure of the Standard Model can be interpreted as the symmetry subgroup preserved by these orthogonal projections of the spiral field, instead of being postulated as an independent abstract symmetry.

The research roadmap adopts modern numerical and analytical techniques: high-precision field equation integration, renormalization group flow analysis, ADM-BSSN numerical relativity simulation, and experimental discriminant tests including anomalous magnetic moment and electric dipole moment measurements, to verify whether the predictions of this geometric unification scheme are consistent with existing observational and experimental data.
