**A falsifiable pure-κ-phase dark-matter prediction in a normalized-helicity unification framework**

We present a falsifiable dark-matter prediction derived from a normalized-helicity unification framework, whose master equation κ̃²+τ̃²=1 closes with machine-zero residuals across all fundamental constants. Dark matter is identified with **pure-κ-phase particles** (internal torsion τ = 0) on the normalized unit helix — gravity-only, with no electromagnetic or weak coupling. Its mass is converged by two independent routes: (i) a geometric-fraction inversion of the Planck Ω_DM/Ω_b (f_DM = 0.8428742, residual 0.0), giving m_DM ≈ 1.68 GeV; and (ii) standard freeze-out relic density with a weak-scale geometric cross section ⟨σv⟩ = λG_F²m_DM², giving m_DM = 1.685 GeV (λ_back = 1.0), consistent with route (i) to ~0.4%. We specify four testable criteria: zero electromagnetic coupling, gravity-only, a mass–curvature relation, and distinguishability from WIMPs (cross section ≈ 0). Underground liquid-xenon experiments are expected to show null nuclear recoils, gamma-ray observatories a low-background dark region, and the mass search should target the 1–2 GeV channel. A nonzero weak/EM cross section, or a mass deviating beyond 3σ from 1.7 GeV, would falsify the claim. A complete reproducible data package is provided (entry point script V4-36, rerunnable with character-identical output). Following the NG-X honesty boundary (V4-40), the structural layer is derived from a geometric machine-zero closure, while the numerical layer (≈1.68 GeV) retains cosmological scale anchors; we do not claim first-principles uniqueness for α or m_e.
